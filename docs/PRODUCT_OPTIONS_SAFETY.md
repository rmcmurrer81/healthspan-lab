# Research-Backed Product Options Safety

Healthspan Lab can help users move from research questions to practical product categories, but product relevance and medical suitability are different concepts.

## Initial scope

Version 2.1 is intentionally conservative.

It may surface non-prescription categories such as:

- home monitoring devices;
- exercise equipment;
- activity-tracking devices;
- sleep-environment products;
- skin-protection products;
- functional measurement devices.

The interface may link to retailer searches such as Amazon or Walgreens.

Healthspan Lab does not endorse a specific retailer, seller, brand, listing, or marketplace review.

## What the matching score means

A product-category relevance score is based on text overlap between:

- active profile research interests and goals;
- confirmed health-context labels;
- the current research question; and
- the category's research tags.

A higher relevance score does not mean the item is medically necessary or proven to extend lifespan.

## Evidence before shopping

Every category includes a research query.

Users should be able to review current source-backed evidence before deciding whether an item is useful.

Evidence strength should never be replaced by:

- retailer popularity;
- star ratings;
- advertising claims;
- affiliate commission;
- profile-match score.

## Lab values

A confirmed lab value must not automatically cause Healthspan Lab to recommend:

- a vitamin;
- a mineral;
- a supplement;
- a medication;
- a hormone;
- a drug dose.

The current system may use the **name of a confirmed measurement** as a research-discovery term, but not its numerical value for automated shopping logic.

## Future ingestible OTC / supplement support

Before personalized ingestible-product suggestions are added, Healthspan Lab should have:

1. medication-list support;
2. interaction screening;
3. allergy and contraindication fields;
4. pregnancy-related safety logic where relevant;
5. kidney/liver safety context where relevant;
6. dose and formulation normalization;
7. evidence-quality rules;
8. pharmacist / clinician escalation language;
9. clear separation between correcting a documented deficiency and general longevity claims.

Prescription drugs, controlled substances, hormones, and high-risk or unregulated products should remain outside ordinary shopping recommendations.

## Retail links

Retail links should be treated as searches, not endorsements.

The app should avoid implying:

- that a listed item is in stock;
- that a seller is trustworthy without verification;
- that the displayed price is current;
- that purchasing the item is medically recommended.

## Affiliate links

If Kira Labs later adds affiliate monetization, the interface should disclose affiliate relationships clearly and should never let commission affect evidence ranking or health relevance.
